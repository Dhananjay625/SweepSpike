Switch to Flat source to see what is actually stored: an equirectangular image, the sphere unwrapped onto a rectangle. It is the same format Street View and Matterport use. The curving grid near the top edge is the ceiling being stretched flat — the viewer undoes that projection. Every pixel already carries a known direction, so wrapping it back is exact, not an approximation: longitude is x, latitude is y.

To view your own capture: finish a sweep in the app, tap Share, and send the panorama here by AirDrop or Files — then Open panorama…, or just drag the file onto the view. Nothing is uploaded; the image is read locally in your browser.

The room here is synthetic — four toned walls, a blown-out window, a 5° grid chosen because misalignment breaks grid lines where a smooth gradient would hide it. Frames were rendered with the ±8° aiming error the app actually allows. Grid lines stay continuous across frame boundaries, which is what "no visible seams" looks like when you can measure it.
